<role>
You are a QA engineer with 5-6 years' experience in agile teams. Before test design starts, you analyse the requirements and raise your questions with the Product Owner or Business Analyst, so that tests are not written on guesses.
</role>

<context>
The product is the Toolshop, an e-commerce demo application built for practising software testing. It has a product catalogue, a cart and checkout, and a customer account area where logged-in users keep a favourites list.

Requirement analysis is the step where a tester reads the requirements and finds what would cause wrong or missing tests. The user story and acceptance criteria below have already been reviewed and accepted. Take them as they stand and read them as the tester who has to design and run tests from them.

The scope is the Product Detail page, the actions a visitor takes on it, and how a visitor reaches it.

A tester looks for five kinds of problem:
- Gap or omission: something a tester needs that is not stated, such as a state, an input, a rule, an error case or a message.
- Ambiguity or vagueness: wording that has more than one reading, or no measure.
- Testability: a behaviour for which the tester cannot state an observable, measurable expected result.
- Inconsistency or contradiction: two statements, or two terms for the same thing, that disagree.
- Inaccuracy: a statement that looks wrong against the rest of the requirements or how such a feature normally works.

Domain knowledge is the most valuable input here. Use what you know about e-commerce product pages, and about the Toolshop, to find what a tester would need to ask even where the story is silent.
</context>

<user_story>
As a visitor,
I want to view a product's details, add it to my cart, or save it to my favorites,
so that I can purchase it or come back to it later.
</user_story>

<acceptance_criteria>
Feature: Product detail
  As a visitor,
  I want to view a product's details, add it to my cart, or save it to my favorites,
  so that I can purchase it or come back to it later.

  Rule: A visitor can view a product's details

    Scenario: Visitor opens a product's page
      Given the catalogue contains a hammer priced at $14.50
      When a visitor opens the hammer's page
      Then the page should show the hammer's image, name, description and price
      And the page should show the hammer's category and brand

    Scenario: Visitor sees related products
      Given the catalogue contains a hammer and products related to it
      When a visitor opens the hammer's page
      Then the page should show the products related to the hammer

    Scenario: Visitor opens a product that is not in the catalogue
      Given the catalogue does not contain combination pliers
      When a visitor opens the page for combination pliers
      Then the visitor should be told that combination pliers could not be found

    Question parked: Confirm the exact wording of the message shown when a product cannot be found with the Product Owner or Business Analyst.

  Rule: Quantity must be between 1 and 999,999,999

    Scenario: Quantity starts at 1
      Given the catalogue contains a hammer that is in stock
      When a visitor opens the hammer's page
      Then the selected quantity should be 1

    Scenario Outline: Quantity within the limits is accepted
      Given a visitor is viewing a hammer that is in stock
      When the visitor changes the quantity to <entered quantity>
      Then the selected quantity should be <entered quantity>

      Examples:
        | entered quantity |
        | 1 |
        | 2 |
        | 50 |
        | 999999999 |

    Scenario Outline: Quantity below 1 resets to 1
      Given a visitor is viewing a hammer that is in stock
      When the visitor changes the quantity to <entered quantity>
      Then the selected quantity should reset to 1

      Examples:
        | entered quantity |
        | -1 |
        | 0 |

    Scenario Outline: Quantity above 999,999,999 resets to 999,999,999
      Given a visitor is viewing a hammer that is in stock
      When the visitor changes the quantity to <entered quantity>
      Then the selected quantity should reset to 999999999

      Examples:
        | entered quantity |
        | 1000000000 |
        | 1000000001 |

  Rule: A visitor can add an available product to the cart

    Scenario: Visitor adds a product to the cart
      Given the hammer is in stock
      And the visitor has selected a quantity of 1
      When the visitor adds the hammer to the cart
      Then the visitor should see the message "Product added to shopping cart."
      And the cart should hold 1 hammer

    Scenario: Visitor adds the selected quantity to the cart
      Given the hammer is in stock
      And the visitor has selected a quantity of 3
      When the visitor adds the hammer to the cart
      Then the visitor should see the message "Product added to shopping cart."
      And the cart should hold 3 hammers

    Scenario: Visitor opens a product that is out of stock
      Given the hammer is out of stock
      When a visitor opens the hammer's page
      Then the visitor should see "Out of stock" for the hammer
      And the hammer should not be available to add to the cart

    Question parked: Confirm whether adding a product that is already in the cart increases its quantity or is blocked with the Product Owner or Business Analyst.
    Question parked: Confirm what the visitor should see when the quantity exceeds the available stock with the Product Owner or Business Analyst.
    Question parked: Confirm how a rental item is handled on this page, since the out-of-stock rule excludes rental items, with the Product Owner or Business Analyst.

  Rule: A visitor can save a product to their favorites

    Scenario: Logged-in visitor saves a product to their favorites
      Given a visitor is logged in as Jane Doe
      And Jane Doe's favorites do not contain the hammer
      When Jane Doe saves the hammer to her favorites
      Then the hammer should appear in Jane Doe's favorites
      And Jane Doe should see the message "Product added to your favorites list."

    Scenario: Logged-in visitor saves a product that is already in their favorites
      Given a visitor is logged in as Jane Doe
      And Jane Doe's favorites already contain the hammer
      When Jane Doe saves the hammer to her favorites
      Then Jane Doe's favorites should contain the hammer once
      And Jane Doe should see the message "Product already in your favorites list."

    Scenario: Visitor who is not logged in saves a product to their favorites
      Given a visitor is not logged in
      When the visitor saves the hammer to their favorites
      Then the visitor should see the message "Unauthorized, can not add product to your favorite list."

    Question parked: Confirm whether a product that is already in a user's favorites is shown as saved on its page (for example a filled heart) with the Product Owner or Business Analyst.
    Question parked: Confirm whether a maximum number of favorites applies with the Product Owner or Business Analyst.
</acceptance_criteria>

<instructions>
1. Read the user story and the acceptance criteria as the tester who must design tests from them.
2. Go through the five kinds of problem one at a time. For each, list every finding that would affect test design or the expected result.
3. For each finding, quote the line of the story or acceptance criteria it comes from, write the question, say who it is directed to, and rate its criticality with a short reason.
4. Do not repeat a question that is already parked in the acceptance criteria.
5. If a kind of problem has no finding, write "None found" for it. Do not invent findings to fill it.
6. Before finishing, check each finding: the quote is exact, the question can be answered by the person it is directed to, it is not already parked, and the criticality fits the definitions. Correct any that fail. Give only the final table.
</instructions>

<constraints>
- Write every finding as a question. Anything you believe about how the Toolshop works, but the story and criteria do not say, is asked as a question and never stated as fact.
- Directed to is one of: Product Owner, Business Analyst, Developer, UX designer.
- Criticality is High, Medium or Low, followed by a colon and what it blocks for the tester. High: the tester cannot state an expected result, or the feature may be built wrongly. Medium: a test can be written only with an assumption. Low: a clarification that does not change an expected result.
- Leave Response empty and set Status to Open on every row. The team fills these in later.
- The length of the output follows from what the requirements need. There is no target number of findings.
</constraints>

<output_format>
One table with these columns, one row per finding, grouped by category in the order of the list above:

| ID | Category | Requirement or AC | Question | Directed to | Criticality and what it blocks | Response | Status |

For a category with no findings, add one row: the ID is "-", the category is named, and the other columns say "None found".
</output_format>
