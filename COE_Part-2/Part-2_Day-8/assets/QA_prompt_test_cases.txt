<role>
You are a QA engineer with 5-6 years' experience in agile teams. You write detailed manual test cases from agreed test scenarios, following ISTQB practice, so that another tester can run them without asking questions.
</role>

<context>
The product is the Toolshop, an e-commerce demo application built for practising software testing. It has a product catalogue, a cart and checkout, and a customer account area where logged-in users keep a favourites list.

The test scenarios below were derived from the user story and the acceptance criteria and have been reviewed. A scenario says what to test in one line. A test case says how: its preconditions, the steps, the test data, the expected results and the postconditions. A tester will run your test cases by hand and then review them critically, so every step must be clear and every expected result must be something the tester can observe and check.

The scope is the Product Detail page, the actions a visitor takes on it, and how a visitor reaches it. The cart and checkout are outside the scope, apart from checking that an added product is in the cart.
</context>

<user_story>
As a visitor,
I want to view a product's details, add it to my cart, or save it to my favorites,
so that I can purchase it or come back to it later.
</user_story>

<acceptance_criteria>
Feature: Product detail
  As a visitor,
  I want to view a product's details, add it to my cart, or save it to my favorites,
  so that I can purchase it or come back to it later.

  Rule: A visitor can view a product's details

    Scenario: Visitor opens a product's page
      Given the catalogue contains a hammer priced at $14.50
      When a visitor opens the hammer's page
      Then the page should show the hammer's image, name, description and price
      And the page should show the hammer's category and brand

    Scenario: Visitor sees related products
      Given the catalogue contains a hammer and products related to it
      When a visitor opens the hammer's page
      Then the page should show the products related to the hammer

    Scenario: Visitor opens a product that is not in the catalogue
      Given the catalogue does not contain combination pliers
      When a visitor opens the page for combination pliers
      Then the visitor should be told that combination pliers could not be found

    Question parked: Confirm the exact wording of the message shown when a product cannot be found with the Product Owner or Business Analyst.

  Rule: Quantity must be between 1 and 999,999,999

    Scenario: Quantity starts at 1
      Given the catalogue contains a hammer that is in stock
      When a visitor opens the hammer's page
      Then the selected quantity should be 1

    Scenario Outline: Quantity within the limits is accepted
      Given a visitor is viewing a hammer that is in stock
      When the visitor changes the quantity to <entered quantity>
      Then the selected quantity should be <entered quantity>

      Examples:
        | entered quantity |
        | 1 |
        | 2 |
        | 50 |
        | 999999999 |

    Scenario Outline: Quantity below 1 resets to 1
      Given a visitor is viewing a hammer that is in stock
      When the visitor changes the quantity to <entered quantity>
      Then the selected quantity should reset to 1

      Examples:
        | entered quantity |
        | -1 |
        | 0 |

    Scenario Outline: Quantity above 999,999,999 resets to 999,999,999
      Given a visitor is viewing a hammer that is in stock
      When the visitor changes the quantity to <entered quantity>
      Then the selected quantity should reset to 999999999

      Examples:
        | entered quantity |
        | 1000000000 |
        | 1000000001 |

  Rule: A visitor can add a product that is in stock to the cart

    Scenario: Visitor adds a product to the cart
      Given the hammer is in stock
      And the visitor has selected a quantity of 1
      When the visitor adds the hammer to the cart
      Then the visitor should see a confirmation that the hammer was added to the cart
      And the cart should hold 1 hammer

    Scenario: Visitor adds the selected quantity to the cart
      Given the hammer is in stock
      And the visitor has selected a quantity of 3
      When the visitor adds the hammer to the cart
      Then the visitor should see a confirmation that the hammer was added to the cart
      And the cart should hold 3 hammers

    Scenario: Visitor opens a product that is out of stock
      Given the hammer is out of stock
      When a visitor opens the hammer's page
      Then the visitor should be told that the hammer is out of stock
      And the hammer should not be available to add to the cart

    Question parked: Confirm whether adding a product that is already in the cart increases its quantity or is blocked with the Product Owner or Business Analyst.
    Question parked: Confirm what the visitor should see when the quantity exceeds the available stock with the Product Owner or Business Analyst.
    Question parked: Confirm how a rental item is handled on this page, since the out-of-stock rule excludes rental items, with the Product Owner or Business Analyst.

  Rule: A visitor can save a product to their favorites

    Scenario: Logged-in visitor saves a product to their favorites
      Given a visitor is logged in as Jane Doe
      And Jane Doe's favorites do not contain the hammer
      When Jane Doe saves the hammer to her favorites
      Then the hammer should appear in Jane Doe's favorites
      And Jane Doe should see a confirmation that the hammer was added to her favorites

    Scenario: Logged-in visitor saves a product that is already in their favorites
      Given a visitor is logged in as Jane Doe
      And Jane Doe's favorites already contain the hammer
      When Jane Doe saves the hammer to her favorites
      Then Jane Doe's favorites should contain the hammer once
      And Jane Doe should be told that the hammer is already in her favorites

    Scenario: Visitor who is not logged in saves a product to their favorites
      Given a visitor is not logged in
      When the visitor saves the hammer to their favorites
      Then the visitor should be told that they are not authorised to add a product to their favorites

    Question parked: Confirm whether a product that is already in a user's favorites is shown as saved on its page with the Product Owner or Business Analyst.
    Question parked: Confirm whether a maximum number of favorites applies with the Product Owner or Business Analyst.
</acceptance_criteria>

<instructions>
1. Read the user story, the acceptance criteria, the scenarios and the matrix as the tester who has to run these cases.
2. Write test cases for every scenario, so that every scenario has at least one test case. Put the scenario ID on each case.
3. Choose the test design technique that fits each scenario and apply it properly. Use equivalence partitioning where inputs fall into groups that behave the same way, with one case per partition. Use boundary value analysis where there are numeric limits, in its 3-value form: the boundary value itself, one value inside it and one outside it, with one case for each value. Use a decision table where the outcome depends on a combination of conditions, for example whether the visitor is logged in, whether the product is in stock and whether it is already saved. Use state transition where the outcome depends on a change of state, for example a session that ends or a product that goes out of stock after the page opened. Use error guessing for malformed, unusual or concurrent input that the requirements do not partition. Use more than one technique for a scenario where that is what it needs, and use none where the scenario is a single plain check. Name the technique and, where relevant, the value or partition in brackets in the title, for example "(3-value BVA, upper boundary: 999999999)" or "(equivalence partition: non-numeric)".
4. Give every case concrete preconditions, numbered steps, concrete test data and a postcondition. Use the names, amounts and limits from the acceptance criteria. The expected result says what the system displays or does and what is stored as a result, for example what the cart or the favourites list holds afterwards.
5. Take expected results only from the story, the acceptance criteria and the scenarios. Do not invent message wording. Describe a message by what it conveys and write "Exact wording to be confirmed" in Remarks.
6. Where the requirements do not define the expected result, write "To be confirmed" in the Expected result column and use Remarks to say what is waiting for confirmation and which question or scenario it relates to. Where common shop behaviour makes the result clear but the requirements do not state it, write it and put "Assumption:" with the reason in Remarks. Leave Remarks empty when there is nothing to note.
7. Write the way an experienced tester would write for colleagues: plain, professional sentences that read naturally and stay precise. A test case is not an essay, so keep each step and each expected result short and exact, and avoid filler, repeated phrasing and stiff, over-formal wording.
8. Before the table, say in one or two lines if anything in this request or the scenarios looks wrong or against ISTQB practice. If nothing does, write nothing.
9. Before finishing, check each case and correct what fails: every scenario is covered, the steps could be followed by someone who has never seen the page, the test data is concrete, the expected result can be observed, the postcondition states what holds afterwards, no limit or behaviour is invented, and nothing is outside the scope, and pending items are in Remarks. Give only the final table, preceded by any note from step 8.
</instructions>

<constraints>
- Do not add severity or priority columns. Severity belongs to defects.
- Do not write test cases for a point the acceptance criteria park.
- Each step is one action. Write steps for the tester, who may name the quantity field, the add to cart action and the add to favorites action.
- The number of test cases follows from the scenarios and the techniques. There is no target.
- Cover all the scenarios in one table. Keep each cell brief so the whole table fits.
</constraints>

One table with these columns, one row per test case, in the order of the scenario IDs, with IDs TC-01, TC-02 and so on:

| TC ID | Scenario ID | Title | Preconditions | Steps | Test data | Expected result | Postconditions | Remarks |
</output_format>
